# Local internal rotations, full Gram correction, and a target-sphere trace

2026-10-16. The target is the full two-dimensional exponent-one sharp
Lieb–Thirring problem, with arbitrary finite rank and no spatial symmetry.
This note does not establish its sharp constant. It investigates a family of
variations different from the independent spatial dilations in R25, computes
their full orthogonality correction, and carries a proposed negative-trace
argument through that correction. The apparent dimension-dependent negative
trace does not survive as an unconditional conclusion.

## 1. Normalization and the constrained quadratic form

Let real orthonormal orbitals satisfy

    H phi_i=-e_i phi_i,
    H=-Delta-a rho,  rho=sum_i phi_i²,  a>0,
    e_1>=...>=e_N>0.

For an optimizing candidate these occupy the lowest N eigenvalues and have a
gap above them. Let P project onto their span and Pi=I-P. Write Phi for the
column of orbitals, E=diag(e_i), and

    T=sum_i ||grad phi_i||², F=int rho²,
    J_a=T-(a/2)F.

Stationarity under common spatial scaling gives J_a=0, hence

    T=(a/2)F=sum_i e_i=:S.

The desired inequality is J_a>=0 for a=M_c. A strictly improving optimizer
would have a=2/C_N<M_c. All formulas below keep the unknown a explicit.

For a real horizontal tangent psi_i perpendicular to Ran(P), the exactly
orthonormal chart (Phi+s Psi)(I+s² Psi*Psi)^(-1/2) gives

    J_a(s)=s² Q(psi)+o(s²),
    Q(psi)=sum_i <psi_i,(H+e_i)psi_i>
                  -2a int(sum_i phi_i psi_i)².             (1)

The first term is strictly positive on nonzero horizontal vectors because
of the occupied spectral ordering and gap. The question is whether the
negative density term must dominate for a putative subcritical optimizer.

## 2. What arbitrary spatially varying internal rotations actually vary

Let K(x) be a smooth real antisymmetric N by N matrix, initially of compact
support, and form the pointwise rotation U_tilde(s,x)=exp(sK(x))Phi(x).
It preserves the total density exactly, but generally changes the global
Gram matrix. Define

    eta_i=(K Phi)_i,  B_ji=<phi_j,eta_i>,
    G(s)=U_tilde(s)^*U_tilde(s),  G'(0)=B+B^T.

The polar correction U_hat(s)=U_tilde(s)G(s)^(-1/2) is exactly orthonormal.
After discarding the constant internal rotation, its physical tangent is

    psi_i=Pi eta_i.

Since Phi dot eta=0 pointwise,

    d(x):=sum_i phi_i psi_i=-Phi(x)^T B Phi(x)
                         =-(1/2)Phi(x)^T G'(0)Phi(x),
    rho_hat'(0)=2d=-Phi^T G'(0)Phi.                       (2)

Moreover Tr G'(0)=2 int Phi dot eta=0. Thus, although K(x) is an arbitrary
matrix-valued function, all of its first-order density responses after
orthogonalization lie in the finite-dimensional space

    { Phi(x)^T C Phi(x) : C=C^T, Tr C=0 }.                (3)

This is a limitation on this variation class, not on the full orbital
Hessian. The local rotation contributes to density at first order only
through its induced global Gram correction.

There is no lost first-order Gram direction in this statement. When the
lowest occupied orbital is strictly positive, the map K -> G'(0) is onto
all real traceless symmetric matrices. To prove this, suppose a symmetric C
annihilates its range. Testing arbitrary compactly supported skew matrices
implies C Phi(x) is parallel to Phi(x) at every point. The continuous map
Phi/|Phi| lies in a single eigenspace of C: its domain is connected and
Phi never vanishes because phi_1>0. Orthonormality forces the span of its
image to be all of R^N. Hence C is scalar, which is precisely the annihilator
of the traceless matrices. Finite dimensionality then gives surjectivity.
The resulting quadratic function in (3) can still have an algebraic kernel;
no injectivity of C -> Phi^T C Phi is claimed.

If G'(0)=0, then d=0 and (1) gives Q>=0, strictly unless the horizontal
tangent vanishes. Therefore a local rotation which preserves the Gram
matrix to first order cannot itself supply a negative direction.
There is also a finite version: any exactly orthonormal competitor P_new
with rho_new=rho obeys

    J_a(P_new)-J_a(P)=Tr H(P_new-P)
       >= gap * Tr(Pi P_new)>=0.

This is the min–max principle, with gap=lambda_(N+1)(H)-lambda_N(H)>0.
A successful local-rotation argument must exploit the nonzero density
response caused by Gram correction, rather than density-preserving motion.

## 3. Target-sphere conformal variations: the tempting negative trace

Put

    r=sqrt(rho), n=Phi/r, |n|=1,
    A=int rho |grad n|², T_r=int |grad r|², T=T_r+A.

The ground-state positivity makes these pointwise definitions smooth locally;
all fields below are in H1 by their homogeneous Lipschitz dependence on Phi.
For alpha=1,...,N, use the tangent field on the internal unit sphere

    eta_i^(alpha)=r(delta_(i alpha)-n_i n_alpha).

It is perpendicular to Phi pointwise, and hence is generated by a local real
rotation. These are the standard target-sphere conformal directions, now
applied to the occupied orbital direction n(x).

For any pointwise tangent vector Y with n dot Y=0, the orbital equations give

    Q_0(rY)=int rho [|grad Y|²-|grad n|²|Y|²
                         +Y^T E Y-(n^T E n)|Y|²],
    Q_0(eta)=sum_i <eta_i,(H+e_i)eta_i>.

Indeed H r=-r[|grad n|²+n^T E n]. For
Y^(alpha)=e_alpha-n_alpha n, the exact identities

    sum_alpha |Y^(alpha)|²=N-1,
    sum_alpha |grad Y^(alpha)|²=2|grad n|²

and int rho n n^T=I imply

    sum_alpha Q_0(eta^(alpha))=(3-N) A.                 (4)

At N>=4 this is strictly negative whenever the occupied direction varies.
It is NOT a constrained negative trace: these eta change the Gram matrix.
The remaining sections calculate, rather than drop, the entire correction.

## 4. Complete summation of the Gram terms

Define the finite moments and the orientation-energy moment

    v_i=int r phi_i=int rho n_i,
    C_(alpha i j)=int rho n_alpha n_i n_j,
    z_i=int r phi_i |grad n|².

The projection coefficients are

    B^(alpha)_(ji)=delta_(i alpha)v_j-C_(alpha i j).

Let psi^(alpha)=Pi eta^(alpha) and d_alpha=Phi dot psi^(alpha). Direct
spectral projection in (1), without any choice of off-diagonal gauge, yields

    sum_alpha Q(psi^(alpha))
      =(3-N)A-sum_(alpha,i,j)(e_i-e_j)(B^(alpha)_(ji))²
                  -2a sum_alpha ||d_alpha||².             (5)

The tensor C is fully symmetric. Its contractions satisfy

    sum_i C_(i i j)=v_j,
    sum_i e_i C_(i i j)=:w_j,
    e_j v_j=w_j+z_j.

Consequently the complete occupied-space correction is

    sum_(alpha,i,j)(e_i-e_j)(B^(alpha)_(ji))²
       =S|v|²-N sum_i e_i v_i²+2 v dot z.                (6)

Thus the exact, fully constrained trace is

    Q_trace=(3-N)A-S|v|²+N sum_i e_i v_i²-2v dot z
                      -2a sum_alpha ||d_alpha||².         (7)

The sign of the correction cannot be inferred by sorting the e_i: a given
pair contributes (e_i-e_j)[B_ji²-B_ij²], whose second factor has no fixed
sign. In particular the corrections are not a nonnegative quantity that
could simply be subtracted to preserve the negative sign in (4).

The tensor summation (6) was also checked numerically on independent finite
orthonormal matrices at ranks 2, 3, 4 and 7; discrepancies were below 5e-16.
The proof is the displayed exact contraction, not that floating-point test.

## 5. The correction removes the apparent rank cutoff

There is a useful equivalent form which exposes the cancellation. Set

    q=Pi r, Pr=sum_i v_i phi_i, ||q||²=N-|v|².

Since <r,Hr>=-S-A, one has

    sum_i e_i v_i²=S+A+<q,Hq>,
    v dot z=A-int r q |grad n|².

Substitution into (7) gives the exact formula

    Q_trace=A+2 int r q |grad n|²
                  +S||q||²+N<q,Hq>
                  -2a sum_alpha ||d_alpha||².             (8)

The prefactor 3-N has disappeared. The last term remains negative, but
there is no dimension-only negative term left. For example, spectral
ordering gives

    S||q||²+N<q,Hq>
      >= [S-N e_(N+1)]||q||²>=0,

where e_(N+1)=-lambda_(N+1)(H), interpreted as zero when the next level is
at the essential-spectrum threshold. The term int r q |grad n|² has no
established sign. It cannot be set to zero: r need not belong to the
occupied span.

The scalar GN inequality does use the desired subcritical condition a<M_c,
but only gives

    F<=C_1 N T_r, C_1=2/M_c,
    A<=S[1-M_c/(aN)].                                  (9)

This is an UPPER bound on the orientation energy. The proposed instability
would require a sufficiently large lower bound on the negative density
response in (8) relative to the projected spectral cost. Inequality (9)
does not supply it. Thus neither the eigenvalue ordering nor scalar GN
completes the sign argument, and (4) cannot produce a universal finite-rank
upper bound after the actual constraints are imposed.

There is a valid limited spectral consequence. Put Delta=e_1-e_N. Since
sum_alpha ||B^(alpha)||_F²<=sum_alpha ||eta^(alpha)||²=N(N-1), equations
(4)-(5) and positivity of the projected spectral cost imply, for N>3,

    (N-3) A <= Delta N(N-1).                           (10)

This bound actually concerns orthonormal lowest eigenfunctions of any common
scalar potential; it does not use a<M_c or solve the nonlinear problem.
A very narrow occupied spectral cluster cannot support arbitrarily large
internal directional kinetic energy. It supplies no rank bound because A
can become small and Delta is not uniformly controlled by the target
hypotheses. It should not be described as an advance to the sharp constant.

## 6. A bounded rank-two rotation which avoids the old fourth-moment issue

For a rank-two candidate let u=phi_1>0, v=phi_2, delta=e_1-e_2>0 and
write u=r cos(theta), v=r sin(theta), with -pi/2<theta<pi/2. The exact
angle equation is

    -div(rho grad theta)=(delta/2)rho sin(2theta).

A local rotation eta=f(-v,u) has symmetric occupied projection after the
gauge int rho f=0. For this gauge its constrained quadratic form is

    Q=int rho |grad f|²+delta int f²(v²-u²)-2a int d²,
    d=-Phi^T B Phi.

Unlike the unbounded ratio-polynomial variations in R24, choose the bounded
f=sin(2theta)=2uv/rho. It automatically satisfies int rho f=0 by
orthogonality and defines an H1 variation without any frequency-ratio or
fourth-moment assumption. Let

    C=int u²v²/rho,
    D=int uv(u²-v²)/rho.

Then B=[[-2C,D],[D,2C]] and d=2C(u²-v²)-2Duv. Testing the angle equation
with G(theta)=2theta+(1/2)sin(4theta), whose derivative is
4cos²(2theta), evaluates the derivative term exactly. The result is

    Q=delta int rho[theta sin(2theta)
                         -(1/2)sin²(2theta)cos(2theta)]
                  -8a int[C(u²-v²)-Duv]².                (11)

All integrals are finite and the test is admissible at every binding-frequency
ratio. A negative right side would exclude that exact candidate. No universal
sign has been obtained from the stationary equations, the occupied ordering,
or a<M_c. In particular the quartic contrast can be small for nearly
separated occupied directions, so it has not been replaced by a positive
mass-only lower bound. This supplies a genuine all-ratio allowable test,
not a proof that the test always succeeds.

## 7. Outcome

The sphere-map approach did produce a negative uncorrected trace, but the
exact Gram calculation removes the purported dimension-based contradiction.
The resulting finite-rank formulas are valid for strongly overlapping,
nonradial families and contain no separated-core approximation. They show
precisely which projected-amplitude and frequency terms must be overcome.
Local rotations preserving both density and Gram cannot lower the energy;
those changing the Gram can only access the density variations (3) to first
order. The bounded rank-two test (11) removes a technical moment restriction
from one class of earlier tests, but no compact subcritical optimizer is
excluded by the general hypotheses alone. The full endpoint remains open
in this calculation.
