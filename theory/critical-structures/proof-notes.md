# Research continuation: proof dependencies

Date: 2026-10-08.
Mainline: critical focusing dispersive systems; thresholds, coherent structures, and dynamics.

This record concerns the analytic results in the new manuscript.Earlier numerical counterexamples were not rerun in this continuation and are not premises of the new concentration-energy theorem.

## Concentration energy and conservative evolution

The central argument was checked independently of the author's wording. At mass at most the sharp homogeneous mass, the homogeneous energy is nonnegative. Bounded energy for a dispersion differing by a bounded multiplier consequently forces bounded homogeneous energy. If the kinetic energy diverged, normalization gives a sharp interpolation near-optimizer. Strong compactness then computes the bounded correction's limit from the angular limiting multiplier. This proves the claimed lower limiting energy and boundedness of every strict sublevel below it. Dilated exact optimizers prove sharpness above that level. No blowup trajectory follows merely from an unbounded sublevel.

The quartic, dimensions 1--3, flow result includes its local theory: the power has locally Lipschitz second derivative, H2 embeds in Linfinity and W1,4, and the real multiplier is unitary on H2. Fourier-projected evolution conserves the actual energy and converges locally in H2. Thus the concrete global theorem does not hide a Strichartz estimate for an arbitrary anisotropic symbol.

For the rational example with m=4 and q=6, the high-frequency symbol is (1+epsilon)|xi|^4 and the bounded deficit is epsilon|xi|^4/(1+|xi|^6), tending to zero. The upper sharp mass is (1+epsilon)^(N/4) times the lower mass. Endpoint negative-energy data exist and all satisfy the global boundedness theorem in N=1,2,3. This is a modified-dispersion result, not a claim about the pure quartic equation.

The set-stability proof retains the one-sided mass restriction at the upper endpoint. The exact scale-curvature formula and its coefficients were checked. The low-threshold asymptotic imports the preceding optimizer-selection and correction-energy convergence results, explicitly stated in the text; ordinary strong Hs convergence is not used as a substitute for high-moment tightness.

## Spectral pair and endpoint degeneracy

Checked conventions J=-i, the momentum normalization, the real action Hessian and its parameter derivatives. The finite-dimensional reduction is an exact complement-resolvent calculation, analytic in lambda and continuous in velocity. Its scalar Schur complement is even, hence analytic in lambda squared. The zero-frequency value follows from charge scaling, avoiding differentiation of an uncontrolled profile remainder. The coefficient of lambda squared subtracts the translation coupling. The resulting denominator is positive because the scaling derivative cannot be a linear combination of translations for a nonzero L2 function.

The theorem retains real-profile nondegeneracy, a differentiable profile branch, a fixed symmetry kernel, and norm-continuous complementary inverses. It proves a real pair for m>2 and an imaginary pair near zero for 1<m<2. It does not prove nonlinear instability in every phase space, exclude all other spectrum in the latter case, or cover the degenerate m=2 coefficient by extrapolation.

The endpoint implications were checked from E=d-theta dot grad(d) and critical charge scaling. A negative-energy mass minimum forces H e_omega=0; a lower-mass zero-energy profile forces H theta=0. These are null-vector statements, not the assertion that H is the zero matrix. Fredholm solvability produces the neutral Jordan chain under the stated slice assumptions. The standing complex profile with zero velocity but nonzero momentum is explicitly retained as an exception to one endpoint argument.

## Hilbert-valued compactness and quantitative polarization

Checked the dimension-independent sharp inequality and equality polarization using strict density convexity and rotations of real components. The local compactness proof triangularizes only a finite prefix of internal coefficient vectors for each sequence element; this permits a genuine unitary extension even in infinite dimension. Compact scalar local embeddings then control the remaining spatial coefficients in operator norm. The vector Brezis--Lieb and Hartree density splittings, followed by strict binding of mass and kinetic energy, give strong compactness.

The final quantitative polarization inequality controls distance to arbitrary rank-one fields zeta q, not distance to the scalar optimizer manifold. Its transverse Taylor remainder is o(||Z||Hs^2) for every kappa>1; for Hartree it is quartic. Scalar interpolation expands the loss of mass and kinetic energy in the main component, and scalar phase coercivity controls the transverse components after internal alignment. No scalar real-sector nondegeneracy is inferred. The arbitrary-field inequality, the common-frequency profile rigidity, and the negative-energy mass gap are separate claims with their stated scopes.

## Anisotropic transport

Checked the exact core equality for the kinetic commutator as an equality of quadratic forms. The defect operator is not asserted to vanish pointwise on a supported function. The homogeneous Fourier-Schur bound is uniform under spatial rescaling. Nonlinear local and Hartree terms have principal contribution -4m times the attractive energy; moving-center current contributes -2 c' dot P. Together with the kinetic term this gives 4mE-2 c' dot P.

The literal exterior |D|^(m/2)u density is related to the cutoff energy by a small cutoff commutator. This gives the final spatial error exponent min(m/2,2/m). The time-window radius is fixed during each integration. Jensen's inequality gives the uniform positive exterior average for negative energy and zero momentum. The exact drift limit uses the two-sided estimate; arbitrary uniformly localizing centers are regularized using the nonlinear core and energy-space temporal continuity.

Smooth-approximation assumptions are explicit. The theorem supplies a necessary transport mechanism for a bounded global trajectory, not an exclusion of every indefinitely replenished exterior mechanism.
