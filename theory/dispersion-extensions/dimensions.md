# dimension derivation and new numerical checks

Date: 2026-10-07. This note reports floating-point numerical evidence, not a new rigorous counterexample theorem. In particular, a negative inverse pairing does not by itself prove an exact standing state, all required spectral gaps, or a nonlinear traveling branch.

## Outcome

The existing 42-point integer-dimension continuation table (six radial families, N=2,...,8) contains exactly three negative translation responses: N4/family1, N5/family1, and N3/family4. The N4 case was the previous proof target. This round independently refined the previously unvalidated N3 and N5 leads, checked them with a different discretization, and explored two additional excited-vortex families in N2.

- **N3:** a four-strong-ring excited radial state of the genuinely mass-critical equation with exponent 8/3 has robust numerical I = **−1323.39702629**. This is the strongest new dimension-specific lead. It survives three origin radii, three truncation radii, two outer boundary conditions, and independent radial finite differences. The weakest numerical spectral gap is in the imaginary degree-one channel, with a preconditioned gap about 0.00667.
- **N5:** a one-strong-ring radial state of the genuinely mass-critical equation with exponent 8/5 has robust numerical I = **−194759.8229**. Its inverse problem is less well conditioned, with an L² eigenvalue about −0.00340. One overly tight response solve failed and is explicitly excluded from the convergence evidence.
- **N2:** no negative response was found. New charge-one vortices with three and four substantial radial peaks have response matrices approximately 78.18055 times the identity and 219.24665 times the identity. These extend the previous fundamental-vortex tests but do not establish any nonexistence result.

All computations below use the correct dimension-dependent power. No four-dimensional cubic was reinterpreted as a three-dimensional model.

## Equations and normalization

Write p=8/N. The real radial standing equation is

$$
 \Delta_N^2q+q-|q|^p q=0.
$$

For the degree-one translation response write $\psi(x)=x_1V(r)$. Then

$$
 [\Delta_{N+2}^2+1-|q|^p]V=q'/r,
$$

and the full-space inverse pairing is

$$
 I=\frac{|S^{N-1}|}{N}\int_0^\infty q'V r^N\,dr.
$$

The independently computed quadratic-form version is

$$
 I_{\rm form}=\frac{|S^{N-1}|}{N}\int_0^\infty
 \left[(\Delta_{N+2}V)^2+(1-|q|^p)V^2\right]r^{N+1}\,dr.
$$

For a subsequently proved nondegenerate traveling branch, the exact identities would give
$P_1(Q_v)=2Iv+o(v)$, $E(Q_v)=Iv^2/4+o(v^2)$.
For noninteger powers, a sufficiently differentiable implicit-function theorem is appropriate; the polynomial real-analytic argument used for N4 should not simply be copied.

The energy of the standing profile itself must be zero. Tiny negative standing energies in the tables below are discretization diagnostics, not counterexamples.

## New N3 refinement

The seed is [round2_radial_mode4_dim3.npz](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round2_radial_mode4_dim3.npz). Its nonlinearity exponent is p=8/3. The strong outer extrema occur near radii 2.10, 4.625, 7.85, and 11.875, outside the central peak; subsequent small oscillatory tails are not counted as strong rings.

[round3_dimensions_refine.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_dimensions_refine.py) removes the singular origin by starting at r=epsilon with a ten-term even Taylor series. Its first two coefficients are unknown BVP parameters, and the remaining coefficients follow recursively from the actual power p. The outer boundary is either q(R)=q'(R)=0 or the two-dimensional decaying linear stable subspace generated by $r^{-(N-2)/2}K_{(N-2)/2}(e^{i\pi/4}r)$ and its conjugate. The response uses the corresponding effective dimension N+2. Stable boundary conditions on the forced response are a finite-radius approximation, checked against the clamped computation; they are not an exact tail proof.

Each norm and pairing is evaluated by ten-point Gaussian quadrature on the common refinement of the two actual collocation meshes, including the Taylor interval [0,epsilon]. The archived NPZ files contain meshes, state values, polynomial coefficients, origin coefficients and boundary matrices.

## Independent finite differences

The degree-one radial unitary transform gives an operator on L²(dr),

$$
 H=-\partial_r^2+\frac{N^2-1}{4r^2},\qquad
 L=H^2+1-|q|^{8/N}.
$$

[round3_dimensions_fd.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_dimensions_fd.py) uses centered second differences for H, forms its square, and solves the resulting symmetric pentadiagonal system. Dirichlet conditions for the transformed H at the endpoints induce a Navier-type outer condition for H². This differs from the collocation outer conditions and is harmless only in the numerical large-domain comparison given here; no infinite-domain error enclosure is claimed. The source is $r^{(N-1)/2}q'$, and the Riemann sum includes the full sphere factor |S^{N−1}|/N.

The responses converge toward the collocation values and remain negative at every tested mesh. This is independent numerical evidence, not an extrapolation used as a proof. At the finest h=.005, fourth-order matrix conditioning magnifies floating-point cancellation: the raw linear residual norms are approximately 3.00e−4 (N3) and 3.47e−3 (N5). Thus the finest-grid eigenvalue digits should not be overinterpreted. The h=.01 domain comparison is more reliable for truncation sensitivity.

## N3 spectral channels and the main certification obstacle

The correct linearizations are

$$
 L_+=\Delta^2+1-\tfrac{11}{3}|q|^{8/3},\qquad
 L_-=\Delta^2+1-|q|^{8/3}.
$$

The symmetry-restricted branch needs even angular degrees for L+ and odd degrees for L−. [round3_dimensions_channels.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_dimensions_channels.py) computed all channels through degree seven at h=.02 and .01, R=40, using shift-invert eigenvalues and an independent symmetric banded count of negative eigenvalues. These are finite matrices, not interval spectral certificates.

The L− degree-one eigenvalues nearest zero are approximately −.0104503 and +.0133092 at h=.01. The BS operator has leading eigenvalues

    2.11778679, 1.22818199, 1.00667322, .98846775, .27273739, ...

The L+ degree-zero BS operator starts with

    16.72369325, 6.06304235, 4.17627164, 3.66673131,
    3.63211818, .95678468, .73945938, .50671695, ...

This pinpoints the weakest obligation: certify the closely spaced eigenvalues on both sides of one in the degree-one BS channel, then transfer its gap to the exact standing profile. The N4 shortcut of subtracting all above-one Ritz squares from one coarse HS-square bound may fail here: the sum of squares of the remaining below-one eigenvalues is itself plausibly greater than one. A finite-dimensional block containing the near-one directions, with an independently bounded remainder and coupling, is a better certification target. This is a methodological consequence of the actual computed spectrum, not merely a call for a finer grid.

[round3_dimensions_highchannels.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_dimensions_highchannels.py) extended the calculation. For L+, negative modes remain at degrees 8,10,12,14; the last is about −.265544. Degrees 16,18,20 have no negative eigenvalue in these matrices. L− degrees 9,11,13,15 are positive. Values above one in a truncated-box computation should not be read as a whole-space gap exceeding one: the whole-space essential spectrum begins at one.

For N3 and degree l≥1, with lambda=l(l+1), integration by parts gives

$$
 \int r^2|\Delta_lf|^2dr
 =\int r^2|\Delta_0f|^2dr+2\lambda\int|f'|^2dr
 +\lambda(\lambda-2)\int r^{-2}|f|^2dr.
$$

It supplies a conservative large-degree route. Sampling the current profile gives

    sup r^4 (|q|^(8/3)−1) ≈ 18151.06,
    sup r^4 ((11/3)|q|^(8/3)−1) ≈ 122291.80.

These numbers suggest sufficient tails at odd degrees ≥13 and even degrees ≥20 using lambda(lambda−2). They are sampled bounds, not certified inequalities; the exact-profile transfer also remains necessary. One can alternatively validate positivity at degree 5 for L− and degree 16 for L+, and use monotonicity of the angular forms.

N3 has one useful simplification: the free radial Green kernels use half-integer Bessel orders l+1/2, so their special functions reduce to finite combinations of exponentials and rational functions. The new nonpolynomial potential |q|^(8/3), especially its behavior at radial zeros, must nevertheless be enclosed. It is C² at the zeros, allowing second-derivative quadrature bounds, but it is not a polynomial that can be silently fed through the N4 rational-polynomial verifier.

## N5 refinement, including a failed run

The seed is [round2_radial_mode1_dim5.npz](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round2_radial_mode1_dim5.npz), with p=8/5. Successful new runs are:

| R | epsilon | BC | tolerance | I | I_form−I |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 | .010 | stable | 2e−9 | −194759.822892550 | 4.79e−6 |
| 50 | .020 | stable | 1e−9 | −194759.822881989 | −1.83e−6 |
| 60 | .020 | clamped | 2e−9 | −194759.822895755 | 4.89e−6 |

The last two radii have negligible endpoint amplitudes (about 1.2e−16 and 2.1e−19 for q). For the R=50 successful run, q(0)=14.21457760882965 and M=39950.20622764649. The standing energy is −9.05e−9, again a consistency defect around exact zero.

A different R=50 run with epsilon=.005 and tolerance 3e−10 **failed for the response**, exhausting refinement with reported residual 1.17e−4. Its standing solve converged. Although its I estimate is close to the others, that failed response is not counted as a successful refinement. It is retained in the JSON and NPZ for transparency. The replacement epsilon=.020, tolerance1e−9 run converged for both equations. The finite-difference script uses only the converged standing component of the original R=50 file, not its failed response component.

The degree-one finite-difference operator has one negative eigenvalue near −.00340; the positive spectrum begins near one. This small negative eigenvalue explains the much larger inverse response and sensitivity. N5 is therefore a legitimate further lead but a less attractive next certification target than N3 on conditioning grounds.

## New N2 structures actually tested

Previous experiments had covered real radial rings, several nonradial sign geometries, and fundamental vortices of charges one through four. Here two excited **charge-one** vortex families were constructed using a fixed-disk nonlinear eigenmode continuation, so the candidates have additional radial nodes.

Set $Q=e^{i\theta}rU(r)$. The exact N2 quintic profile equation reduces to

$$
 \Delta_4^2U+\omega U-r^4U^5=0.
$$

[round3_vortex_eigen.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_vortex_eigen.py) starts from clamped charge-one biharmonic Bessel eigenfunctions on R=20, fixes U(0), and continues amplitude with unknown frequency. Positive-frequency states are rescaled to frequency one, with a scaled outer radius above42. Labels `mode1` and `mode3` describe the starting linear eigenmode index, not a guaranteed nonlinear nodal count; the actual radial peaks and mass distinguish the outputs.

[round3_vortex_fft.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_vortex_fft.py) then solves the full complex elliptic equation on a 256×256 periodic square of side64 and computes both velocity directions using the real-linear Hessian. The phase and two translation kernels are projected out, and the full 2×2 response matrix is reported.

| starting mode label | substantial radial peak radii | mass | stationary elliptic max residual | response eigenvalues |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.538, 5.070, 9.235 | 318.230543592 | 1.28e−9 | 78.18055050, 78.18055051 |
| 3 | 1.432, 4.675, 8.448, 12.761 | 536.416933186 | 1.72e−8 | 219.24664934, 219.24665091 |

Both nonlinear solves and all four GMRES response solves converged. Absolute response residuals are between 1.3e−6 and1.8e−6; symmetry-related off-diagonal matrix entries are below 5e−9. The response signs are firmly positive at these tested approximations. No expensive refinement or broader parameter search was pursued after this negative finding, since neither family approached zero response.

An initial attempt to continue charge continuously from m=0 to m=1 failed at m=.125,.0625,.03125. That failure alone supplies no nonexistence evidence. The fixed-integer-charge eigenmode route above succeeded and supersedes that failed numerical route. Its failed logs are retained rather than omitted.

The reason this round does not claim an N2 advance to a counterexample is concrete: every accepted new response is positive. The earlier one-dimensional multipulse-to-dimension continuation also folded before N=2; pseudo-arclength checks had already ruled out a mere small-step Newton problem on those particular branches. None of these bounded searches rules out other N2 excited geometries or larger-velocity sign changes.

## Reproduction files and scope

Main code:

- [round3_dimensions_refine.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_dimensions_refine.py): N3/N5 Taylor-origin standing and response BVPs; use argument `5 extra` for the additional successful N5 run.

- [round3_dimensions_bs.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_dimensions_bs.py): numerical preconditioned spectra.
- [round3_vortex_eigen.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_vortex_eigen.py), [round3_vortex_fft.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_vortex_fft.py): the two new N2 vortex families; run with starting-mode argument 1 or3.
- [round3_vortex_excited.py](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_vortex_excited.py): the initial failed charge-continuation route, kept for audit.

Data and summaries have the same `round3_dimensions_*` and `round3_vortex_*` prefixes. [round3_dimensions_existing_inventory.json](../../computations/dispersion-extensions/numerical_evidence/round3_dimensions_existing_inventory.json) freezes the 42 previously saved integer-dimension records used for comparison. All NPZ files contain floating-point data, not rational or interval certificates.

No direct nonlinear traveling wave was constructed in N3 or N5 in this round, and no exact-profile or spectral certificate was completed there. The completed result of this subtask is a carefully refined, independently corroborated numerical lead in N3, a second lead in N5, and two explicitly unsuccessful new N2 families, with identified obstacles to a rigorous next step.
