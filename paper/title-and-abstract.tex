\title{Negative-Energy Global Solutions and Traveling-Wave Mass Gaps in Mass-Critical Dispersive Equations}
\author{Shuxing Yang}
\date{28 October 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Negative energy is central to blowup criteria for focusing dispersive equations, but its consequences beyond radial symmetry depend on the interaction between dispersion and momentum. We construct nonradial, global negative-energy solutions of the mass-critical biharmonic Schr\"odinger equation in dimensions three and four by continuing excited standing waves with negative momentum response. Computer-assisted estimates certify the required profile and spectral inputs. In four dimensions, nonlinear gluing produces zero-total-momentum, forward-global solutions with uniformly bounded Sobolev norms, both as separating two-wave states and as a single coherent core coupled to momentum-cancelling radiation. For a prescribed core, we determine the exact infimum of the radiation mass compatible with negative total energy. Complementing these constructions, we prove a uniform positive gap between the critical mass and the mass of every negative-energy traveling profile for real-even, positive elliptic homogeneous dispersion. A quantitative residual estimate extends this obstruction to approximate profiles without spatial-moment assumptions. The same phase-coercivity mechanism yields a uniform intrinsic-energy mass gap for a three-dimensional cubic mixed-dispersion model with nonnegative transverse quadratic corrections. Spectral instability, conserved-quantity geometry, and localized transport further constrain the resulting dynamics. The constructions rule out an unconditional nonradial extension of negative-energy blowup or growup, while the mass-gap results separate this coherent mechanism from the critical variational threshold.
\end{abstract}
