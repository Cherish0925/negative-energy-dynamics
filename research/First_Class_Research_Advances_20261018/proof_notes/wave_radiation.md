# A single negative-energy wave with momentum-cancelling radiation

Date: 2026-10-18. This is a new analytic construction derived from the project's existing four-dimensional profile and spectral certificates. It does not recertify those numerical inputs and makes no priority claim. The conclusion below is an exact PDE construction, not only an energy budget.

## 1. Result and relation to the existing two-wave construction

Use

\[
iu_t=\Delta^2u-|u|^2u\quad\text{on }\mathbb R^4,
\qquad
M(u)=\|u\|_2^2,\quad
P(u)=\operatorname{Im}\int\bar u\nabla u,
\quad E(u)=\tfrac12\|\Delta u\|_2^2-\tfrac14\|u\|_4^4.
\]

Let the existing certified branch be

\[
u_v(t,x)=e^{it}Q_v(x-vte_1),\qquad
P(Q_v)=-q_v e_1,\quad q_v>0,
\qquad E(Q_v)=-\frac{vq_v}{8},
\]

where, as \(v\downarrow0\),

\[
q_v=2|I|v+O(v^3),\qquad I<0,\qquad M(Q_v)=M(Q)+O(v^2).
\]

The prior construction used two full excited profiles and had mass \(2M(Q_v)\). Here the compensating momentum can be carried by dispersive radiation of mass \(O(v^{2/3})\).

**Construction theorem, relative to the existing profile/spectral certificates.** For every sufficiently small fixed nonzero \(v>0\), there are \(T_v>0\) and an exact solution in \(C([T_v,\infty);H^{10})\) satisfying

\[
u(t)=e^{it}Q_v(\cdot-vte_1)+e^{-it\Delta^2}r_{+,v}+o_{H^{10}}(1),
\]

and

\[
P(u)=0,\qquad E(u)<0,\qquad
\sup_{t\ge T_v}\|u(t)\|_{H^{10}}<\infty,
\qquad
M(u)=M(Q)+O(v^{2/3}).
\]

Time translation gives a forward-global solution starting at zero; its displayed asymptotic formula then has the corresponding constant phase and spatial shift, and scattering state \(e^{-iT_v\Delta^2}r_{+,v}\). These changes preserve every conserved quantity. The same construction works in any preassigned finite graph Sobolev space \(H^{2+4k}\) with \(k\ge2\), with its starting time allowed to depend on that choice.

In particular, the limit of these masses is \(M(Q)\), rather than the \(2M(Q)\) limit of the symmetric pair. This does not approach the ground-state critical mass \(M_c\): the stationary excited-state mass \(M(Q)\) remains the reference value. No stability of an open set of initial data is asserted.

The proof has three ingredients: a center evolution bound extracted from the already certified saturated index; a compact-velocity, high-order radiation approximation; and a polynomially weighted final-state contraction.

## 2. The missing linear estimate follows from the saturated index

In the rotating and translating frame let

\[
L_v=\Delta^2+1+iv\partial_1-V_v,
\qquad V_v h=2|Q_v|^2h+Q_v^2\bar h,
\qquad B_v=-iL_v.
\]

Everything is interpreted on a real Hilbert space. The prior four-dimensional certificates give, for all sufficiently small nonzero \(v\),

\[
n_-(L_v)=36,\qquad \dim E^u(B_v)=\dim E^s(B_v)=31,
\]

and the five-dimensional kernel generated by phase and translations. The free symbol \(|\xi|^4+1-v\xi_1\) is positive, so the action Hessian is a compact perturbation of a positive elliptic operator and zero is isolated for the generator.

Let \(U_{\omega,v}\) denote the frequency/velocity family and

\[
d(\omega,v)=S_{\omega,v}(U_{\omega,v}),\qquad
S_{\omega,v}=E+\frac\omega2M-\frac12v\cdot P.
\]

Critical scaling gives

\[
d(\omega,v)=\omega d(1,v\omega^{-3/4}),
\qquad
d(1,v)=d(1,0)-\frac I2|v|^2+O(|v|^4).
\]

At \(\omega=1\), the leading axial \((\omega,v_1)\) Hessian is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
-3Iv^2/8&Iv/2\\ Iv/2&-I
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose determinant is \(I^2v^2/8>0\); transverse entries are \(-I+O(v^2)>0\). Thus \(d''\) is positive definite for small nonzero \(v\). Equivalently this follows directly from the existing bordered momentum formula: \(D_vP=2I\mathrm{Id}+O(v^2)<0\) and \(\kappa=-4I|v|^2+O(v^4)>0\).

The five parameter derivatives span a generalized kernel on which

\[
\langle L_v\partial_{a_i}U,\partial_{a_j}U\rangle=-d''_{ij},
\qquad a=(\omega,v).
\]

They therefore account for five negative directions. Invertibility of \(d''\) says that the symmetry Jordan chains have length exactly two. The paired stable and unstable subspaces account for another 31 negative directions: the Hamiltonian energy form on their sum has inertia \((31,31)\). All 36 negative directions are exhausted.

On the invariant complement of the symmetry generalized kernel and the hyperbolic spaces, the energy form is positive and coercive. Positivity follows from the exhausted index, and coercivity follows from the positive essential spectrum, finite-dimensional kernel, and exclusion of that kernel. Its evolution preserves this positive form, hence is uniformly bounded. On the generalized kernel the evolution grows at most linearly. Consequently

\[
\|e^{tB_v}P_c\|_{H^2\to H^2}\le C_v(1+|t|),
\]

with the usual exponential bounds in the proper directions on \(E^s,E^u\). Since \(B_v\) is autonomous and elliptic,

\[
\|h\|_{H^{2+4k}}\asymp\sum_{j=0}^k\|B_v^j h\|_{H^2},
\]

and the invariant projections commute with \(B_v\). The same center bound therefore holds in \(H^{10}\).

This step uses the exact unstable count, not merely finiteness of the Morse index. It does not require a dispersive estimate for the linearized generator, threshold-resonance exclusion, or a Fermi golden rule.

## 3. Radiation that has the correct momentum, energy and velocity

Choose a fixed real radial smooth function \(\phi\) supported in the unit ball, with \(\|\phi\|_2=1\). Let

\[
\widehat r_+(\xi)=\mu^{1/2}\sigma^{-2}
\phi\!\left(\frac{\xi-ke_1}{\sigma}\right),
\qquad \sigma=\theta k,\quad 0<\theta<1/4.
\]

With the unitary Fourier transform this has

\[
M(r_+)=\mu,\qquad P(r_+)=\mu k e_1,
\]

and

\[
E_{\rm free}(r_+)=\tfrac12\|\Delta r_+\|_2^2
=\frac{\mu k^4}{2}\,C_\theta,
\quad C_\theta=1+3\theta^2\!\int|\zeta|^2|\phi|^2
+\theta^4\!\int|\zeta|^4|\phi|^2.
\]

The coefficient 3 uses dimension four and radiality of \(\phi\). Set \(\mu k=q_v\). A convenient exact parameterization is

\[
k=(\eta v/4)^{1/3},\qquad
\mu=\frac{q_v}{k},
\]

with fixed \(0<\eta<1\) small enough that

\[
C_\theta\eta<1,
\qquad (1+\theta)^3\eta<1/2.
\]

Then all group velocities in the Fourier support satisfy

\[
|\nabla a(\xi)|=4|\xi|^3<v/2,
\]

while

\[
E(Q_v)+E_{\rm free}(r_+)
=-\frac{vq_v}{8}(1-C_\theta\eta)<0.
\]

The radiation mass is

\[
\mu=q_v(4/\eta v)^{1/3}
=2|I|(4/\eta)^{1/3}v^{2/3}+O(v^{8/3}).
\]

For a monochromatic packet the relation is especially transparent: if its group velocity is \(w=4k^3\), then \(E_{\rm free}=q_vw/8\), and the combined energy is \(q_v(w-v)/8\). The energetic condition and the velocity separation have the same origin. A packet slower than the core can carry the compensating momentum at a smaller energy cost than the magnitude of the core's negative energy.

The construction fixes \(v\) before taking large time. Constants and the required starting time can diverge as \(v\to0\).

### Sharp radiation cost for a fixed coherent profile

For arbitrary free radiation of mass \(\mu>0\) and momentum \(q_ve_1\), Jensen's inequality for \(|\xi|^4\) gives

\[
E_{\rm free}(r_+)\ge\frac{q_v^4}{2\mu^3}.
\]

Therefore negative total energy with this fixed core necessarily requires

\[
\mu>\mu_{\rm crit}(v):=q_v(4/v)^{1/3}.
\]

The \(v^{2/3}\) order is consequently optimal within this fixed-core plus free-radiation asymptotic class.

There is also a matching sufficiency statement from the construction below. For any fixed \(\mu>\mu_{\rm crit}(v)\), set \(k=q_v/\mu\) and \(\eta=4k^3/v<1\). Choose \(\theta>0\) sufficiently small that \(C_\theta\eta<1\) and \(\beta:=(1+\theta)^3\eta<1\). The radiation velocities then lie in \(|y|\le\beta v\), and its separation from the core is at least \((1-\beta)vt\). The WKB proof and the final-state contraction below use only a fixed positive separation coefficient, so every estimate remains valid with \(v/2\) replaced by \((1-\beta)v\). Thus the infimum of the total masses in this specified asymptotic class is exactly

\[
M(Q_v)+q_v(4/v)^{1/3},
\]

and it is not attained at negative total energy. This is not the minimum mass among all negative-energy global solutions; the core is prescribed and the asymptotic decomposition is part of the class definition.

## 4. High-order outgoing approximation with exact velocity separation

A naive use of only the free radiation leaves \(|r|^2r=O_{H^{10}}(t^{-4})\). The linearly growing center Green operator then produces a correction \(O(t^{-2})\), for which the quadratic core remainder is borderline in the same norm. Its coefficient need not be small when the central frequency tends to zero. The following construction removes that borderline without any assertion about nonlinear preservation of Fourier support.

Let \(y=x/t\), let \(\xi(y)\) solve

\[
y=\nabla a(\xi)=4|\xi|^2\xi,
\]

and put

\[
\Phi(y)=y\cdot\xi(y)-|\xi(y)|^4.
\]

All calculations take place on a compact velocity set away from zero. Define

\[
r_{\rm app}(t,x)=t^{-2}e^{it\Phi(x/t)}
\sum_{j=0}^{J}t^{-j}A_j(x/t).
\]

Choose the leading coefficient from stationary phase:

\[
A_0(y)=-\frac{\widehat r_+(\xi(y))}
{\sqrt{\det D^2a(\xi(y))}}.
\]

The minus sign is the four-dimensional stationary-phase factor \(e^{-i\pi N/4}\). This identifies the desired scattering state.

Here is an explicit local recursion for every subsequent coefficient. In the \(y\) variable set

\[
B_0=|\xi(y)|^2,\quad
B_1=-i(2\xi(y)\cdot\nabla_y+\operatorname{div}_y\xi),
\quad B_2=-\Delta_y,
\]

\[
\mathcal L_2=B_1^2+B_0B_2+B_2B_0,
\quad \mathcal L_3=B_1B_2+B_2B_1,
\quad \mathcal L_4=B_2^2.
\]

The order \(t^{-1}\) term in the conjugated fourth-order operator is

\[
B_0B_1+B_1B_0=-i(y\cdot\nabla_y+2).
\]

It cancels the transport of the \(t^{-2}\) prefactor. The eikonal terms cancel as well. The amplitude equation is exactly

\[
i\partial_t A-t^{-2}\mathcal L_2A-t^{-3}\mathcal L_3A
-t^{-4}\mathcal L_4A+t^{-4}|A|^2A=0.
\]

For \(n\ge1\) choose \(A_n\) by

\[
-inA_n-\mathcal L_2A_{n-1}-\mathcal L_3A_{n-2}
-\mathcal L_4A_{n-3}
+\sum_{a+b+c=n-3}A_a\overline{A_b}A_c=0,
\]

with negative-index coefficients and empty sums zero. Every step is a differential polynomial in preceding coefficients divided by the nonzero integer \(n\). Therefore every \(A_n\) is smooth, and all coefficients have support inside the same compact velocity set as \(A_0\). No frequency-support assertion is made for the exact nonlinear solution.

For each fixed Sobolev order \(s\), direct differentiation and the change of variables \(x=ty\) give

\[
\sup_{t\ge1}\|r_{\rm app}(t)\|_{H^s}<\infty,
\qquad \|r_{\rm app}(t)\|_{W^{s,\infty}}\le C_s t^{-2},
\]

\[
\|i\partial_t r_{\rm app}-\Delta^2r_{\rm app}
+|r_{\rm app}|^2r_{\rm app}\|_{H^s}
\le C_{s,J}t^{-J-2}.
\]

Indeed, the first unremoved amplitude power is \(t^{-J-2}\); multiplication by the physical \(t^{-2}\) and the \(L^2\) volume factor \(t^2\) cancel. Spatial derivatives introduce bounded phase frequencies or additional inverse powers of \(t\), so they do not worsen the rate.

Stationary phase on the nondegenerate compact Fourier support, together with integration by parts off its velocity image, gives

\[
\|r_{\rm app}(t)-e^{-it\Delta^2}r_+\|_{H^s}=O(t^{-1}).
\]

Most importantly, \(r_{\rm app}\) is supported where \(|x|<vt/2\), whereas the coherent core is centered at \(vte_1\). Their distance is at least \(vt/2\). Since the core is exponentially localized, every mixed cubic defect is exponentially small in any fixed Sobolev norm.

## 5. Exact nonlinear construction

Put

\[
R(t,x)=e^{it}Q_v(x-vte_1)+r_{\rm app}(t,x).
\]

In the rotating/translating frame write \(u=R+h\). Its correction equation is

\[
\partial_t h=B_vh+F(t)+\mathcal N(t,h),
\]

where, for \(s=10\),

\[
\|F(t)\|_{H^{10}}\lesssim t^{-J-2}+e^{-ct},
\]

and the cubic algebra estimates give

\[
\|\mathcal N(t,h)\|_{H^{10}}
\lesssim (t^{-4}+e^{-ct})\|h\|_{H^{10}}
+\|h\|_{H^{10}}^2+\|h\|_{H^{10}}^3.
\]

The \(t^{-4}\) term is the square of the radiative \(W^{10,\infty}\) bound. Terms linear in radiation and the core are exponentially small. The quadratic estimate uses bounded core and radiation Sobolev norms. The same bounds hold for differences, with the usual sum of correction norms.

Let \(\mathcal G\) integrate center and unstable directions backwards from infinity, and stable directions forwards from a large time \(T\), with stable datum zero. The center estimate gives, for \(L>2\),

\[
\left\|\int_t^\infty e^{(t-s)B_v}P_cf(s)\,ds\right\|_{H^{10}}
\le C_L\|s^Lf(s)\|_{L^\infty_sH^{10}}t^{2-L}.
\]

Hyperbolic integrals obey stronger exponentially localized bounds. With

\[
\|h\|_{X_J}=\sup_{t\ge T}t^J\|h(t)\|_{H^{10}},
\qquad J\ge3,
\]

the map \(h\mapsto\mathcal G(F+\mathcal N(h))\) maps a fixed ball into itself and contracts when \(T\) is large. More explicitly, on a ball of radius \(A\), the linear radiation term has norm bounded by \(C T^{-2}A\), and the quadratic term by \(C T^{2-J}A^2\). The radius is selected to absorb \(\mathcal GF\); only then is \(T\) increased. No smallness of the stationary-phase constant is required.

This proves an exact solution on \([T,\infty)\) with

\[
\|u(t)-R(t)\|_{H^{10}}=O(t^{-J}).
\]

Time translation makes it a forward-global solution with initial time zero, with the corresponding constant phase and center shift in the asymptotic wave and the time-shifted scattering state. It has uniformly bounded \(H^{10}\) norm.

## 6. Exact conserved quantities

The asymptotic decomposition is strong in \(H^{10}\), the coherent core and radiative approximation have separating supports/tails, and \(\|r_{\rm app}\|_4\to0\). Therefore conservation and asymptotic additivity give the exact identities

\[
M(u)=M(Q_v)+\mu,
\quad P(u)=P(Q_v)+P(r_+)=0,
\quad E(u)=E(Q_v)+\tfrac12\|\Delta r_+\|_2^2<0.
\]

These identities are not approximate and do not require tuning the finite-time correction. The scattering state fixes them at infinity.

Finite variance is not included in the theorem above. It should follow by increasing \(J\) and solving the position-commutator equation in a weighted norm, but that extension is kept separate from the completed unweighted construction. The new result already improves the mass of a zero-momentum, negative-energy, bounded forward-global solution from two excited cores to one core plus vanishing radiation.
