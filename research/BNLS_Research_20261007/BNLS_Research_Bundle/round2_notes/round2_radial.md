# Round 2: radial continuation, negative-inertia discovery, and exact approximation certificates

Date: 2026-10-07. The decisive numerical lead is in dimension four, not dimension two. This note distinguishes numerical discoveries from the exact, finite arithmetic checks subsequently performed. The parent research thread is responsible for assembling the remaining spectral/Newton/implicit-function arguments into a complete theorem.

## Outcome

For the mass-critical cubic equation on R^4, an excited real radial standing profile Q with one strong outer ring satisfies numerically

- Q(0) = 6.721690385775376;
- Delta Q(0) = -34.40828790507565;
- M(Q) = 5317.792241997677;
- I = <partial_1 Q, (Delta^2+1-Q^2)^(-1) partial_1 Q> = -273.473861876526.

The negative sign persists under independent discretizations, four domain sizes, clamped versus linear stable-manifold tail conditions, and regular-origin Taylor reconstruction. A collaborating agent directly solved full complex traveling profiles and independently recovered negative energies. Those direct computations remain floating-point calculations; exact arithmetic certificates for an approximate radial profile are listed below.

## 1. How the previously missing radial excited profiles were obtained

Direct Gaussian-ring Newton seeds had failed. A fixed-disk nonlinear eigenmode continuation succeeds:

    Delta_N^2 q + omega q - |q|^p q = 0,

with unknown omega and prescribed center amplitude q(0). At small amplitude, initialize with a clamped radial biharmonic eigenfunction, a combination of Bessel J_0 and I_0. Continue the amplitude, then rescale each positive-frequency profile to omega=1.

In N=2, p=4, this produced radial excited states with zero through twelve strong rings. The well-decayed zero-through-nine-ring states all had positive translation response. Some truncated high-ring states on insufficient domains had negative responses, but their large standing-energy defect and large outer amplitude correctly identified them as finite-domain artifacts. They were not retained as counterexamples.

Continuing actual N=2 profiles in dimension, keeping p=8/N, found the N=4 one-ring negative response. Another numerical lead is the N=3 four-ring profile, I approximately -1323.397, but the N=4 cubic case was prioritized because of its polynomial nonlinearity and simpler profile.

Scripts and exploratory data:

- `round2_radial_eigencontinue.py`, `round2_radial_eigen_results_mode*.json`;
- `round2_radial_integerdims.py`, `round2_radial_integerdim_results.json`;
- `round2_radial_mode1_dim4.npz` is the original N=4 lead.

The old one-dimensional same-sign multipulse route was also tested more carefully. First continuing the power to p=4, then continuing dimension, again stalled before N=2. Pseudo-arclength continuation crossed the folds rather than stopping there: the three-pulse branch reached d approximately 1.2617643 before turning back toward d=1; the fourteen-pulse branch reached d approximately 1.0611311 before turning back. Thus that particular obstruction was not merely a Newton step-size failure.

## 2. Equations and sign conventions

The standing equation is

    Delta_4^2 Q + Q - Q^3 = 0.

Use Y=(Q,Q',Z,Z'), where Z=Delta_4 Q:

    Q' = Y_1,
    Y_1' = Z - 3 Y_1/r,
    Z' = Y_3,
    Y_3' = Q^3 - Q - 3 Y_3/r.

The translation inverse is in angular degree one. Write

    Psi(x)=x_1 V(r),
    (Delta_6^2+1-Q^2)V=Q'/r.

Consequently

    I=(pi^2/2) integral_0^infinity Q'(r)V(r)r^4 dr.

The independent quadratic-form check is

    I=(pi^2/2) integral_0^infinity [(Delta_6 V)^2+(1-Q^2)V^2]r^5 dr.

For a sufficiently regular, nondegenerate traveling branch,

    U_v = Q - i v Psi + O(v^2),
    Delta^2 U_v + U_v + i v partial_1 U_v - |U_v|^2 U_v=0,

one has

    P_1(U_v)=2v I+O(v^3),
    E(U_v)=v P_1(U_v)/8=v^2 I/4+O(v^4).

These signs follow directly from the specified PDE and momentum P=Im integral conjugate(U) grad(U). In particular, a rigorously established branch with I<0 gives negative-energy global traveling solutions. No Galilean symmetry is used.

## 3. Numerical refinement, independent verification, and limitations

The exact decaying linear radial tail for the standing equation is generated by

    r^(-nu) K_nu(exp(i*pi/4) r), nu=(N-2)/2,

and its conjugate. Orthogonal annihilators of these two state vectors give the linear stable tail boundary conditions used for comparison. For the inhomogeneous V equation, homogeneous stable conditions are an approximation; comparison with clamped conditions on increasing domains makes this tail approximation numerically negligible, but is not itself an exact tail certificate.

On R=30,40,60,100, at collocation tolerance 3e-9, clamped and stable boundary conditions gave I approximately -273.47386188673, with differences below 4e-11. An overly tight singular-origin solve eventually hit floating-point mesh refinement pathologies. It was not misreported as converged.

This was fixed by starting at epsilon>0 with an explicit regular power series and two unknown leading coefficients. With epsilon=.005,.01,.02, R=40 or 50, and tolerance 1e-10, all standing and linear BVP solves converged. Results agreed at roughly 12 digits:

    I=-273.473861876526,
    E(Q) approximately -7.6e-11,
    ||Delta Q||_2^2+||Q||_2^2-||Q||_4^4 approximately 4e-11.

The latter tiny standing-energy number is a numerical consistency check around the exact identity E(Q)=0, not a negative-energy counterexample by itself.

Full collocation nodes, state values, derivatives, cubic coefficients, and origin series are saved in `round2_radial_taylor4_R40_e0.010.npz` and the corresponding other-radius files. The stored PPoly coefficient array is already in canonical order; reconstruct numerically with `PPoly.construct_fast(c, x, axis=1)`. A raw collocation cubic is not an H^4 candidate and is not used as the certified approximation.

Independent finite differences use the unitary radial transform on the degree-one channel:

    A=(-d^2/dr^2+15/(4r^2))^2+1-Q^2 on L^2(dr).

At mesh sizes .04,.02,.01,.005, the inverse quadratic forms were approximately

    -272.577684, -273.249669, -273.417825, -273.458882,

converging toward the collocation value. The matrix has one negative eigenvalue, approximately -0.0626264, and its computed positive spectrum starts near the essential threshold 1. Thus the negative response is not a near-zero numerical pole. These finite difference eigenvalues are diagnostics, not rigorous spectral enclosures.

For the five low angular channels surviving the axial/parity restriction, numerical spectra are comfortably away from zero. Files `round2_radial_channels4_results.json` and `round2_radial_negative_eigen_l*_beta*.npz` contain the data. Exact spectral validation is handled separately by the other agents.

## 4. Frozen exact H4 approximation

The canonical approximate profile is the file

    round2_radial_H4_exact_h1_16.json

with SHA-256

    c50a6f98682ce5f5bef9ab62ae2a93ba06b91522c8f3e29b859ac521afb8f627.

Its definition is exact:

1. Nodes are rational r=j/16 for j=0,...,512, followed by r=36.
2. Every stored hexadecimal floating-point jet is interpreted as its exact binary rational number.
3. Each interval carries the unique degree-at-most-seven polynomial matching the two endpoint jets (derivatives zero through three), with the interval length computed as the exact difference of rational nodes.
4. At r=0, the first and third derivatives are zero.
5. At r=36, derivatives zero through three are all zero, and the function is zero beyond r=36.

The common exact jets make the profile globally C^3. Its radial extension belongs to H^4(R^4). The extra odd powers allowed in the first polynomial begin at r^5, so they cause no origin singularity in the fourth derivatives.

Do not replace this exact definition by rounded expanded coefficients. The shared loader `round2_radial_exact_common.py` reconstructs the rational polynomials without a numerical solver.

## 5. Executed exact residual certificate

For the canonical profile q, the exact radial residual is

    F=q''''+6q'''/r+3q''/r^2-3q'/r^3+q-q^3.

On each polynomial interval, form exactly

    H=r^3 F=r^3 q''''+6r^2 q'''+3r q''-3q'+r^3(q-q^3).

Its coefficients are rational. Bernstein coefficients, with three exact dyadic subdivisions per cell, bound |H|. At the origin, the first three coefficients vanish exactly, so division by r^3 is polynomial and harmless. Elsewhere integrate the bound against r^(-3). All positive sums and products use outward Decimal arithmetic at 80-digit precision. The angular area uses the rational upper bound 2(355/113)^2>2pi^2.

The executed result is

    ||Delta^2 q+q-q^3||_2^2 < 5.851831603e-8,
    ||Delta^2 q+q-q^3||_2 < 0.000242 = 121/500000.

The final comparison against 0.000242 is checked as a strict rational squared bound. The square-root display is rounded upward separately.

Files:

- `round2_radial_canonical_residual.py`;
- `round2_radial_canonical_residual_certificate.json`, including all per-cell bounds.

This is a rigorous bound on an explicitly defined H^4 function. It is not merely a reported numerical solver residual.

## 6. Executed comparison and norm certificates

On a common refinement, exact Hermite polynomial composition and Bernstein bounds give

    ||q_canonical-q_previous||_infinity < 1.415e-9,
    ||q_canonical||_infinity <= 6.721690385775376,
    ||q_canonical||_Z^2 < 10635.586291 < 104^2,

where ||q||_Z^2=||Delta q||_2^2+||q||_2^2. The Z bound uses the exact radial identity

    integral (Delta_4 q)^2 r^3 dr
      = integral [r^3(q'')^2+3r(q')^2] dr,

so its integrands are ordinary polynomials. The stated Z upper bound uses 355/113 for pi and is slightly coarser than the parallel agent's tighter angular constant.

## 7. Executed uniform high-angular coercivity certificate

Let c_l=l(l+2), and let beta=3 for the real linearization or beta=1 for the imaginary linearization. For every degree l radial component,

    integral |Delta_l f|^2 r^3 dr
      = integral [r^3(f'')^2+(3+2c_l)r(f')^2+c_l^2 r^(-1)f^2] dr.

Exact polynomial Bernstein checks give, for the canonical q,

    beta r^4 q(r)^2 <= (9/10)(r^4+c_l^2),

for beta=1,l>=5 and beta=3,l>=6. Therefore

    Delta_l^2+1-beta q^2 >= (1/10)(Delta_l^2+1)

as quadratic forms, uniformly in all these high angular channels. This preconditioned gap is stronger and more useful than merely asserting a positive L^2 lower bound.

Additional certified bounds are

    sup r^4(q^2-1) <= 239.507497,
    sup r^4(3q^2-1) <= 1183.830299.

Files: `round2_radial_highangular.py`, `round2_radial_highangular_certificate.json`.
